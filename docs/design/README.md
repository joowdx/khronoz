# khronoz — design (draft, 2026-09-08)

Single-word models, plural tables, ULID keys, Postgres. Every table carries `agency_id`; the agency is the tenant. Global defaults belong to the platform agency row, never to null. Integrity is enforced in Postgres, see 07-constraints.md.

The office that sent your invitation is now **the administrator** — sign-in, forgot-password, the users empty state and `auth.invited` say so — and prose describing the customer says what the office does: an office's *timekeeping staff*, a Philippine *government office*, a government *personnel system*. 25. Decided 2026-09-09: `Group` and `Member` are dropped, and `employees.status` is replaced by `employees.tags`, a json array of free-form agency labels that no rule reads. Groups and tags were the same idea twice — both were "how you pick many employees at once" — and a group additionally carried effective dates it never needed. What is left has three non-overlapping jobs, written out as rule 5 of 01-organization.md: units are the formal structure, one placement at a time through `Deployment`; tags are labels on the employee and the way you select many for an ad-hoc bulk action; a `Team` (decision 26) is the only one of the three that decides what a person is expected to work. A fact you filter by is a tag; a rotation you belong to is a team; `meta` is inert payload nothing filters on. `enum status` went the same way: appointment nature varies by agency and by issuance, no rule in the system reads it, and a fixed set khronoz has to keep current is worse than a label the agency owns. `tags` ships with no shape check yet, recorded as an open item under `employees` in 07-constraints.md.
26. Decided 2026-09-09: `Team` is a named `(schedule, anchor)` cohort — `id`, `agency_id`, `name`, `schedule_id`, `anchor` — specified in 04-scheduling.md and 07-constraints.md and implemented in Milestone 3. Three hospital teams are one schedule and three anchors. A team has no membership table: the rosters carrying its `team_id` *are* its membership, so who was on it in March is answerable from their date ranges, and one employee on two teams at once is already impossible under the roster exclusion constraint. Assigning a team writes one roster per employee, each copying the team's schedule and anchor and pointing back with `team_id`; an ad-hoc set is a tag filter plus select-all, writing rosters with `team_id` null. Either way resolution reads one roster row. A roster's `schedule_id` and `anchor` may differ from its team's, deliberately: `team_id` records where the assignment came from, not a rule about what it produced. The `agency_not_platform` trigger list becomes `employees, units, terminals, teams`; Milestone 2 still applies it to `employees` and `units` only, `terminals` is Milestone 5 and `teams` is Milestone 3.
27. Decided 2026-09-10: `employees.tags` ships **with** a shape check. The column is a jsonb array of distinct, non-empty strings, bounded at 20 elements, enforced in the DDL by `employees_tags_valid` (`CHECK (string_set_valid(tags))`) and `employees_tags_bounded` (`CHECK (jsonb_typeof(tags) <> 'array' OR jsonb_array_length(tags) <= 20)`, written that way because Postgres does not order CHECK evaluation), plus `CREATE INDEX employees_tags ... USING gin (tags)` for `tags ? 'x'`. `string_set_valid(jsonb)` is the shared function `users.permissions`' own check already proved out — array, every element a string, no duplicates, and non-empty elements — so the next jsonb label set reuses it and brings its own bound; the empty array stays legal, being the column default. A per-tag character bound (40) is deliberately the Form Request's, not the database's: the length an agency may type is a UI decision that will change, and a CHECK is the wrong place to keep a changing number. This supersedes the closing sentence of decision 25, "`tags` ships with no shape check yet, recorded as an open item under `employees` in 07-constraints.md" — true when it was written, and closed by the next task of the same milestone; 07-constraints.md's `employees` section carries the live spec and the constraint suite tests both checks. Decision 25 itself stands as written: the log is a dated record of what was decided then and is never rewritten (decision 24).

28. Decided 2026-09-10: `employees.hired_at` and `employees.separated_at` are removed. Deployment ranges are the employment history: an open range is current employment, closed ranges preserve prior service, and a new range after a gap records rehire under the same employee number and DTR trail. A person without a placement is not yet started for timekeeping; tags can distinguish departure from a gap without adding a computation rule. This eliminates the former deployment-containment gap rather than adding a cross-table trigger. Unit-head eligibility requires a non-deleted employee with an open deployment, in both the picker and the two Form Requests. Ending a placement is PATCH `employees/{employee}/deployments` with an inclusive `ends`, implemented by one conditional UPDATE of rows whose `ends IS NULL`; zero affected rows redirects to the employee profile with the error “No open placement to end.” A stale submission must never re-date closed history, and no database constraint would stop that rewrite, so the predicate is essential. The former `SeparateEmployee` action is folded into that controller operation. Removal remains distinct: `RemoveEmployee` closes a started open deployment on bare `today()`, deletes a future open deployment that would otherwise become an empty range, then soft-deletes the employee, all in one transaction. Closed history is retained and continues to restrict removal of its unit. This supersedes the employment-date fields and containment gap in 01/07, and the handoff's instruction to call `SeparateEmployee` from destroy. M2 is unreleased and unpushed, so the original employees migration is edited in place; no upgrade migration is needed.

29. Decided 2026-09-10: `Unit` is renamed **`Workgroup`** — model, `workgroups` table, `deployments.workgroup_id`, `WorkgroupPolicy`, `WorkgroupController`, the `workgroups.*` routes and the Workgroups screen. The reason is a vocabulary collision, not taste: a Philippine agency's hierarchy runs department → division → section → **unit**, so "unit" was simultaneously the container's name and one of the values of its own `kind` column. The collision cannot be fixed on the `kind` side. `kind` is deliberately a plain nullable string with no CHECK and no PHP enum (decision R5 in 00-principles.md) because 06-attendance.md rule 3 resolves the `head` attestation role as "the head of the nearest ancestor workgroup **of the kind the setting names**" — the vocabulary has to stay the agency's, so an agency can always type `kind = 'unit'` and nothing may stop it. Only the container could move. `unit` is therefore now written into the `kind` example lists (01-organization.md's ERD, 00-principles.md's org-shape row, the `workgroups` migration docblock and the marketing copy), so that a later reader finds the rationale rather than renaming it back. **Accepted risk:** "workgroup" and "team" are near-synonyms in English, and a `Team` (decision 26) is a different thing. Accepted, because the two live in different nav groups — Workgroups under Organization, Teams under Scheduling — and answer different questions: a workgroup is *where you are placed*, one at a time, through `Deployment`; a team is *the rotation you are rostered into*. Stating that here is what stops it being re-litigated. Three catalog names were hand-written and renamed explicitly — the `workgroups_parent_not_self` CHECK, the `workgroups_acyclic` constraint trigger and the `workgroups_acyclic()` function; every other constraint and index name followed automatically from the table and column names. `prepare_organization` now runs `DROP FUNCTION IF EXISTS units_acyclic()` before creating the new function, because `db:wipe` drops tables but never functions and `OR REPLACE` only ever reaches the new name, so without it every database that ran the pre-rename migration would keep a dead `units_acyclic()` for good. `agency_not_platform()`, `string_set_valid(jsonb)` and `permissions_valid(jsonb)` are shared and did not rename. Deliberately untouched: PHPUnit's `tests/Unit` suite and `app()->runningUnitTests()`, which are the test framework's vocabulary and not ours; and 04-scheduling.md's "Civil Security Unit" worked example, which is a real office's own name and the clearest illustration of why the container moved. M2 is unreleased and unpushed, so the migrations were edited in place with no upgrade migration — decision 28's precedent. The wording of decisions 8, 12, 14, 25 and 26, which say "unit", is superseded by this one; the log is a dated record of what was decided then and is never rewritten (decision 24), so read the concern files for the live spec.

30. Decided 2026-09-10: **visibility becomes workgroup-scoped, and the ledger is not tied to a deployment.** Every one of the thirteen permissions is agency-wide today and no policy carries a workgroup dimension, so any holder of `ledgers.view` sees every ledger in the agency; a timekeeper assigned to one workgroup must not see the records an employee made in another. The grain is the **month**, because the ledger is monthly, and the predicate is *overlap*: a timekeeper sees a ledger if any deployment of that employee, in a workgroup they cover, overlaps that ledger's month. A mid-month move therefore shows that month to **both** workgroups, which is accepted — a partial month is still that month's DTR, and the alternative is a form nobody can complete; what the rule exists to prevent is a workgroup seeing months it never held the person. It follows that `ledgers` carries **no** `deployment_id` and no scoping `workgroup_id`: a single FK would hand a split month to exactly one workgroup and break the rule, and a stamped set of workgroups would only cache what the ranges already answer. The ledger may stamp the workgroup covering the last day of the month for CS Form 48's office field, and that field is display-only, never an access input. Three consequences are load-bearing. **Deployment ranges become access control**, so their integrity is a security property rather than a data-quality one — which reclassifies the two defects the 2026-09-10 adversarial review found (a stale end-placement request closing a replacement placement, and `RemoveEmployee` overwriting a concurrently recorded ending) as security bugs to fix before scoping ships. **Month grain dissolves the coverage-gap problem** that day grain would create, where a gap between deployments orphans records no scoped timekeeper could see, attest or correct. And **the workgroup tree has no history** — `workgroups.parent_id` is current state, so subtree scoping means reparenting a section retroactively changes who can see the past; that is a known limitation, left unsolved deliberately because reparenting is rare and deliberate while date-ranging the tree costs more than it returns. Agency-wide reach must be an explicit grant and never the absence of a restriction, because `AgencyScope` is fail-closed and a scoping model that defaults to unscoped would contradict it exactly where it matters. The likely shape is a `workgroup_user` pivot — the permission says what, the pivot says which rows — and whether it is enforced in the application or by Postgres row-level security is open: `khronoz_app` is not superuser, so RLS would genuinely bind and is the only version in which a forgotten `->where()` cannot leak. The first leak to close is not the ledger but `EmployeeController::show`, which loads the entire deployment history unscoped, and `employees/index`, which lists every employee of the agency. The live spec is rule 5 of 02-access.md; nothing here is implemented, and the ledger itself arrives in Milestone 6.

31. Decided 2026-09-10: **a reassignment or detail is a deployment nested inside another, and `parent_id IS NOT NULL` is the whole fact.** `parent_id` null means the row is the employee's substantive placement, where the plantilla item sits; `parent_id` set means the row is a movement — the person works in another workgroup for a period while the substantive placement stays open, because the item never left. A **transfer** is not a movement: the item moves, so the substantive row closes and a new one opens, which is what a plain deploy already does. There is deliberately **no type column**. An earlier draft of this decision proposed `deployments.permanent`, which was doubly wrong: "permanent" is an appointment nature in Philippine government (permanent, temporary, casual, contractual, co-terminous), which decision 25 already made a tag, and the movement's own label varies by office — a provincial LGU says "detail" where the CSC's term of art is "reassignment," for the same department-to-department arrangement. No rule reads the label, so by decision 25's reasoning it is not a column; the presence of a parent is what the system needs to know. **One placement at a time is preserved, not abandoned:** the single exclusion constraint becomes two partial ones on the same key, partitioned by `parent_id IS NULL`, so an employee has at most one substantive placement and at most one movement per date, and a movement may overlap only the placement it departs from — never another movement, since nobody is detailed to two places at once. Partition on the presence of a parent and never on a label: partitioning by kind would let a "detail" and a "reassignment" overlap. `UNIQUE (id, employee_id)` plus a paired FK on `(parent_id, employee_id)` makes "the parent is the same employee" structural rather than a trigger, the same device the whole schema uses for `(x_id, agency_id)`; what remains for a trigger is cross-row and cannot be a CHECK — a movement's range must sit inside its parent's, and a movement's parent must itself be substantive, so there is no detail from a detail. **The three consumers of this nesting resolve it differently and must not be collapsed into one "operative placement" helper**, which an earlier draft of this decision wrongly proposed: `head`, the final attestation, walks up from the **substantive** row, so the mother department signs last; a work suspension reads the **operative** row, the movement if one covers the date and otherwise the substantive one, because a closure declared on the mother workgroup cannot excuse a day worked in the receiving one; and visibility under decision 30 reads **any** overlapping row, so both workgroups see a split month — the mother must, since it attests those months. That last point simplifies decision 30 rather than complicating it: the substantive row staying open is exactly what grants the mother visibility, a transfer closing it is what ends visibility, and the "not superseded by a child" clause an earlier draft proposed for that predicate is wrong and must not be added — you cannot attest what you cannot see. Left **open**, deliberately: under a detail, whether `supervisor` resolves from the receiving workgroup's head who observed the attendance or from the mother's depends on the arrangement, and it needs a real case rather than an invented rule. Also identified while settling this and **not yet built**: there is no way to correct a deployment. The only writes are create and close, so a mistyped workgroup cannot be fixed — and a correction is not a movement, because the row was never true and principle 2's "a change is a new range" does not apply to a mistake. It wants `PATCH employees/{employee}/deployments/{deployment}` carrying the value it expects to replace, which also fixes the stale-write defect the 2026-09-10 adversarial review found. The live spec is rule 7 of 01-organization.md, with the DDL under `deployments` in 07-constraints.md; nothing here is implemented, and it should land with Milestone 3 before rosters reference deployments.

32. Decided 2026-09-10: **a private-sector agency is configured by individual settings, never by a gov/private mode flag, and tier 1 of 00-principles.md splits into universal and regime constants.** khronoz serves both civil-service and Labor Code employers, and the population is not binary: a GOCC created **by original charter** falls under the civil service while one **incorporated under general corporation law** falls under the Labor Code (*PNCC v. Sison*, and DOLE's own Handbook excludes chartered GOCCs from night differential and holiday pay by exactly that wording). A special law that merely *authorizes* later SEC incorporation is not an original charter, and a non-chartered GOCC can still be subject to government compensation laws — so a `private boolean` would mislabel real entities on day one, the same failure decision 25 rejected for `employees.status` and R5 for `workgroups.kind`. Instead each divergence is its own key in `agencies.settings`: `occurrences`, `suspension_charge`, `dtr_template`, `rest_day_after`, `overtime_after`, `overtime_after_weekly`, `retention_years`. Defaults live in code and a stored key is an override, so a fresh agency stores `{}`. Onboarding offers a **preset** — "Civil service" or "Labor Code" — that resolves to those keys and writes them; the preset itself is **never persisted**, exactly as `Preset` for permissions is a code-only bundle the controller resolves into a stored permission array. A chartered GOCC therefore picks Labor Code and flips the DTR template, and no column had to be right about what a GOCC is. Settings are agency-settable through `Permission::ManageAgency`; platform users reach them by entering the agency, so no new mechanism is needed. Only `rest_day_after` can be bounded — `NULL OR BETWEEN 1 AND 6`, stricter allowed and looser refused — and `null` must stay legal because a civil-service agency is genuinely not under Art. 91; since the regime is deliberately not stored, no constraint can distinguish a lawful null from an evasive one. **khronoz enforces the rule an agency configures; it does not adjudicate which law binds them**, which is a liability boundary and not only a schema choice. The second half of this decision corrects an invariant: 00-principles.md's tier 1 listed the 40-hour week, the 18:00–06:00 night window, the 1.25/1.5 multipliers, the COC caps, the habitual thresholds, no-offsetting, semester boundaries and the 22-day month as universal constants changed only by deploy. They are **civil-service** constants. Under the Labor Code the week has no statutory value, the night window is **22:00–06:00** (Art. 86, four hours narrower), overtime starts at 8 or 12 with a 48-hour weekly ceiling, the multipliers compound differently, and occurrence counting does not exist at all. Tier 1 is therefore two sets — 1a universal (the 480-minute ordinary day, and little else) and 1b regime constants selected by the settings above — and an agency configures *which set applies*, never a value inside one. The night window is the sharpest: it changes which minutes are **classified** as night work, so a night-hours total is not portable between regimes, and RA 7305 §18(b) may force a government hospital onto a second boundary at 10 p.m. for its overtime premium. Also corrected: tier 3's "grace minutes" is not a free agency choice, because CSC creates no grace period and a lenient grace policy on a fixed schedule needs legal authority. Sources and the full rule inventory are `../reference/csc-rules.md` and the new `../reference/dole-rules.md`; nothing here is implemented, `rest_day_after` lands with Milestone 3, and the rest with Milestones 6 to 8.

Decided 2026-09-10: **the night boundary is `settings.night_from`, and the window in force is frozen into the workday snapshot.** Decision 32 established that the night window is a regime constant (18:00–06:00 under RA 11701, 22:00–06:00 under Labor Code Art. 86) but its list of settings keys named no way to select it, while 06-attendance.md hard-coded both the `workdays.night` column comment and daily rule 5 to 18:00–06:00. A Labor Code agency would therefore have classified 18:00–22:00 as night work. The stop-time review found this after decision 32 was committed; the log is never rewritten (decision 24), so this decision supersedes decision 32's key list by adding one key rather than amending it. `night_from` defaults to 18:00 for civil service and 22:00 for the Labor Code preset; there is no `night_to`, because both regimes end at 06:00 and a second value nothing varies is a field to keep in sync for no gain. **The window is frozen into the workday's `shift` snapshot**, for exactly the reason the resolved shift is frozen there (rule 2 of 06-attendance.md's recompute section, and principle 3): `workdays.night` is a stored `smallint`, not a report-time derivation, so an agency correcting its regime months later must not silently re-classify minutes already computed and possibly already attested. What makes this affordable rather than fragile is that the stored column is a convenience, never the authority — every punch keeps its full timestamp, so any other boundary is derivable from `actual_at` on demand, which is the mechanism "Across midnight and month end" already relies on for calendar-day night hours. That matters immediately: **one boundary per agency is not enough even inside the civil service**, because RA 7305 §18(b) puts a public health worker's overtime premium at 22:00 while RA 11701 keeps their differential at 18:00, so a government hospital classifies on two boundaries at once. The second is derived, not stored, and `../reference/csc-rules.md` section D carries that as an open item. Nothing here is implemented; `workdays` arrives in Milestone 6.

Decided 2026-09-10: **the daily overtime threshold is the resolved shift's prescribed duration, not a regime constant, and `settings.overtime_after` is only the default a shift inherits.** Decision 32 put "overtime threshold" in 00-principles.md's tier 1b table as a regime constant selected by an agency setting. That is wrong, and wrong in a way that produces false numbers rather than merely mislabelling a concept: the threshold varies *within* a single agency. CSC Resolution 2600838 puts a compressed-week office on a ten-hour day while its standard staff stay on eight, a hospital shift runs twelve, and CSC-DBM JC 2 s. 2015 §8.2.2 starts overtime only beyond the prescribed hours *of the shift*. An engine reading a flat agency-level `overtime_after = 8` would manufacture two hours of overtime on every ordinary ten-hour compressed-week day and four on a twelve-hour shift. The threshold therefore belongs to the shift — **tier 2**, and specifically the shift snapshot rule 2 of 06-attendance.md's recompute section already freezes into each workday, which is also what makes the correction free: principle 1 resolves every arrangement to a shift before a workday is computed, and principle 3 snapshots the expectation, so `excess` was already defined against the shift's own slots rather than against any agency number. Note the taxonomy precisely, because one of the two reviewers got it wrong: tier 2 is platform default rows, of which shifts are one; **tier 3 is agency settings**, so "per-shift data lives in tier 3" is not the correction — `settings.overtime_after` stays a tier 3 key and its only job is seeding a new shift. What *does* remain a tier 1b regime constant is the set of gates and limits applied to minutes already identified as excess, and that cell was independently wrong: JC 2 s. 2015 §10.1's on-time arrival and two-hour minimum are conditions for overtime **with pay**, not the threshold that makes a minute overtime, and the twelve-hour figure caps overtime *pay* on rest days, holidays and special non-working days with the excess converting to compensatory time off (§10.5) rather than capping overtime generally. The row is renamed "overtime gates and limits" to stop the two ideas being read as one. The log is never rewritten (decision 24), so this supersedes decision 32's tier 1b table row rather than amending it. Found by a two-lineage adversarial review on 2026-09-10 that both reviewers independently confirmed against the CSC resolution and the DBM circular; the same review surfaced four defects in the **deriver** that are recorded as open items in 06-attendance.md rather than settled here, because they are a rework of the computation model and not a correction to an invariant — holiday and rest-day work collapsing into `excess`, the missing ISO-week accumulator for DA 02-04's weekly ceiling, `workdays.night` being unable to separate regular from overtime night minutes, and cross-midnight attribution of day status. Left **open** and deliberately not decided: whether Omnibus Rules Book III Rule X §7's "signature or thumbmark … for each daily entry" binds a bundy-clock or biometric employer, which would make `attestations`' monthly `UNIQUE (ledger_id, role)` grain insufficient. The reviewers split on it, §8 lists "filled-up bundy clock cards" separately from the "time books and daily time records" that must be accomplished in ink, requiring a signature per punch would collapse §7's method (a) into method (c), and neither reviewer reached a BWC opinion; `../reference/dole-rules.md` section G carries both readings. Decided 2026-09-11: **the movement model gets two verbs and no correction verb — a wrongly recorded deployment is deleted and re-created, and `Deployment` may never soft-delete.** Decision 31 settled the shape of the nesting but left the write surface underspecified and got one premise wrong. Two actions, and deliberately not a third: **`TransferEmployee`** is the plantilla item moving, so it closes the open substantive row at `starts - 1` and opens a new substantive row, which is the same operation as an initial placement (nothing to close) and as a rehire after a gap (nothing open to close); **`ReassignEmployee`** is the person moving while the item stays put, so it closes nothing and inserts a row with `parent_id` set, inside the parent's range. It is a rename of `MoveEmployee`, whose logic was already right — "move" was only ever ambiguous between the two. The API says exactly what the table says: a boolean `reassignment` on the existing `POST employees/{employee}/deployments`, and **the parent is derived, never sent**, because a reassignment's parent is always the employee's own open substantive placement and the server already holds it. An earlier draft of this decision had the request carry a parent id; that was worse for the same reason a type column is worse than `parent_id IS NOT NULL` — it invites a caller to name a parent the rule does not let them choose. **A correction is a DELETE, not a PATCH**, which supersedes the `PATCH employees/{employee}/deployments/{deployment}` decision 31 asked for: the row was never true, so there is nothing to preserve, and `DELETE employees/{employee}/deployments/{deployment}` re-creates rather than re-dates. That makes two things load-bearing. `Deployment` **must never use `SoftDeletes`**: a soft delete is an `UPDATE`, so the row would keep its `starts` and `ends` and go on occupying the timeline the two partial exclusion constraints index, and the corrected row replacing it would be refused with `23P01` by the very row it corrects — a trap that is invisible from the model and costs nothing to state. And the self-referencing paired FK is written `ON DELETE RESTRICT ON UPDATE RESTRICT` explicitly, since Blueprint does not default to it: a placement with a reassignment nested under it then refuses deletion with `23001` until the reassignment goes first, rather than orphaning it. Also corrected: **`deployments` has exactly one incoming foreign key, itself, and that is permanent.** `ledgers` refuses a `deployment_id` by decision 30, and rosters, workdays and attestations reference it nowhere, so decision 31's "it should land with Milestone 3 before rosters reference deployments" rests on a false premise — `parent_id` still lands first, but because the two partial exclusions replace the single one that would otherwise forbid the nesting, not because anything downstream points at the table. Any future incoming FK is a new decision, not an implementation detail. One non-obvious property found while implementing and recorded because it will otherwise be rediscovered the hard way: **"no reassignment under a reassignment" is a theorem, not an axiom.** Nesting requires containment, two movements of one employee that contain one another necessarily overlap, and `deployments_no_overlapping_movements` refuses overlapping movements before `deployments_nested` is consulted — so the trigger's two "the parent must itself be substantive" limbs have no reachable violation and are depth rather than the guard. They stay, for the reason `workgroups` keeps both `workgroups_parent_not_self` and `workgroups_acyclic`, but repartitioning that exclusion constraint or making it deferrable would promote them to load-bearing. Carried to **Milestone 6**, not built here: a trigger refusing **any write** — insert, re-date or delete — to a deployment overlapping a locked or attested ledger month. It is unbuildable until `ledgers` and `attestations` exist, the hazard has no foreign key and cannot have one (decision 30's visibility predicate reads deployment ranges by *overlap*, and attestations sit on ledgers with no `deployment_id`), and until those tables exist deletion is unconditionally safe. It is recorded in `06-attendance.md`'s open items as well as here, because that is where Milestone 6 will look. The live spec is rule 7 of `01-organization.md` with the DDL under `deployments` in `07-constraints.md`; the DDL, the model and the constraint tests are implemented, the two actions and the DELETE route follow in the same milestone.

Decided 2026-09-11: **a substantive placement may be fixed-term, and "the current placement" therefore means the row covering a date, never the row with no end.** Decision 35 settled the two verbs and the boolean that selects them but said nothing about `ends`, which both paths now carry: a reassignment almost always has one, because a detail runs for a period, and a substantive placement may have one because a Philippine appointment nature can be contractual, casual or co-terminous (decision 25 made the nature itself a tag, but its *last day* is a range boundary and belongs on the row). Accepting that on a transfer has a consequence that is easy to miss and expensive to get wrong: a fixed-term placement which is perfectly current today already carries an `ends`, so `Employee::currentDeployment`'s old `ends IS NULL` predicate would have reported **no workgroup at all** for those people — on the employee profile, in the employees index, in the workgroup headcount — and would have refused to reassign them, since ReassignEmployee resolves its parent from that relation. The predicate is now `starts <= D AND (ends IS NULL OR ends >= D)` with `D` = today, expressed as the `covering()` scope on `Deployment` so it is written once. Note precisely why the obvious middle option is wrong, because it type-checks and reads naturally: **"has not ended yet"** — `ends IS NULL OR ends >= today` without the lower bound — **is not at most one**, since a placement ending in June and its successor starting in July both satisfy it, which would make a `hasOne` return an arbitrary row. Covering a single date is at most one, and `deployments_no_overlap` is the only reason it is; the relation is sound solely because the exclusion constraint forbids two substantive rows sharing a day. `ends` is inclusive throughout (the ranges are built `'[]'`), so a placement closed *today* is still today's placement and stops being current tomorrow. Two further consequences, both implemented here rather than deferred. `RemoveEmployee` no longer handles "the open placement" but **every row that has not finished** — each substantive placement and reassignment whose range has not closed before today — closing what began and deleting what never started, **innermost first**, because a reassignment nested inside a placement must be closed or gone before its parent's range shrinks (`deployments_nested` refuses that with `P0001`) and the paired FK refuses deleting a parent that still has one (`23001`). And its close became an **expected-value `UPDATE`** — `WHERE ends IS NOT DISTINCT FROM :expected` — which is the first half of the fix the 2026-09-10 adversarial review asked for: a read followed by `$row->update()` is `WHERE id = ?` and would overwrite an ending a concurrent request had already recorded, which decision 30 makes a security defect rather than a data-quality one. Zero affected rows is that outcome and not an error: the row is closed either way, just not on this date. A side effect worth knowing about before it looks like a regression: a query-builder update fires no Eloquent model events, so `RemoveEmployeeTest` can no longer inject a failure on `eloquent.updated: Deployment` and proves the same atomicity by failing on the employee delete, which is the action's last write. The remaining half of that review's finding — a stale form closing a *replacement* placement through `EmployeeDeploymentController::update` — is not fixed by this decision and is still owed.

Decided 2026-09-11: **a continuous statutory leave is one exemption carrying a nullable inclusive last day, and a null `until` means one day rather than no end.** `csc-rules.md` flagged this as needing settlement before Milestone 4 designed `exemptions`: RA 11210 grants 105 **continuous** days for a live birth, 60 for a miscarriage or emergency termination, 15 more for a qualified solo parent and 30 unpaid. Because the period is continuous it spans rest days and holidays, so it cannot be entered as a run of per-workday rows without inventing rows for days nobody was expected to work — and it is one authority in one order, not 105 separate decisions. The shape is therefore `exemptions.until`, a nullable `date` that is the **last day, inclusive**, with null meaning the exemption covers only `date`. Three alternatives were weighed and rejected. A separate `leaves` table would need a **second** foreign key on `workdays`, whose `(exemption_id, employee_id)` pair is already designed in `07-constraints.md` for Milestone 6; the range satisfies that key unchanged, because the deriver asks which exemption covers a date and one row answers for all 105 of them. A roster-level construct — suspending or splitting the `rosters` row — is wrong twice over: it would destroy the schedule history of a person on leave, where principle 2 says a change is a new range and a leave is not a change of schedule, and it would file a *person's* leave in the same bucket as an *office closure*, which `suspensions` deliberately keeps employee-free. And nothing here is special to maternity: paternity's seven days, rehabilitation leave, study leave, terminal leave and RA 9710's two months are the same shape, so the range belongs to the `leave` type decision 19 already settled rather than to a new concept. Note precisely what the continuity does **not** require: counting the 105 days is *entitlement* arithmetic, and decision 32 excludes leave balance accounting from v1, so khronoz records the range the order grants — the solo-parent and unpaid extensions included — and never computes or polices the total. Two consequences are load-bearing and each has a constraint. **Null `until` is the opposite of null `ends`** on `deployments` and `rosters`, where it is an open upper bound: `Concerns\CoversDates` must therefore **never** be reused for `exemptions`, since its predicate would make a two-hour pass slip excuse every day of the rest of an employee's career. `Exemption::covering()` is closed on both sides — `date .. coalesce(until, date)` — and the mutation that substitutes the trait's predicate is killed by a test asserting a single-day slip does not cover tomorrow. And a multi-day exemption is **always whole days**, held by `exemptions_span_is_whole_days CHECK (until IS NULL OR starts IS NULL)`: a 10:00–14:00 window repeated across 105 days is not something an order ever means, and a row saying it would make the deriver excuse four hours a day of a continuous leave, under-excusing by an entire statutory entitlement. `exemptions_span_ordered` is strict (`until > date`) so that null is the single canonical spelling of a one-day exemption and no reader needs `coalesce(until, date)` to mean two different things; normalising a caller's `until = date` to null is the writer's job. Implemented with the table, its enum and its constraint tests in this milestone; nothing consumes the range until the deriver in Milestone 6.

Decided 2026-09-11: **`exemptions.until` is NOT NULL, and a one-day exemption carries `until = date`.** This supersedes decision 37's null convention and nothing else in it: a continuous statutory leave is still one row, still not a separate table, still not a roster-level construct, for the reasons 37 gives. What changes is the spelling of a single day. 37 made `until` nullable with null meaning "one day", which read as a convenience and was a trap: `daterange(date, until, '[]')` with a null upper bound is **unbounded above**, so any query or exclusion constraint built the way the rest of this schema builds ranges — and this schema builds four exclusion constraints exactly that way — would have read a two-hour pass slip as excusing every day thereafter. The Eloquent scope was correct either way; the hazard was SQL nobody had written yet, and a comment cannot reach that. `CHECK (until >= date)` replaces `CHECK (until IS NULL OR until > date)`, which keeps 37's canonical form — there is still exactly one spelling of a one-day exemption, it is just the obvious one — and `exemptions_span_is_whole_days` becomes `CHECK (until = date OR starts IS NULL)`. Three things get better and one gets worse. `Exemption::covering()` loses its `coalesce(until, date)` and becomes two plain comparisons, so there is no null semantics for a reader to misremember; the failure mode moves from silently over-excusing, which under this system means unearned pay and a false DTR, to a loud 23502 on a writer that forgot a column; and an exclusion constraint over `(employee_id, daterange(date, until, '[]'))` becomes *expressible*, which it was not before — still not wanted, since a morning pass slip and an afternoon CTO are one ordinary day, but no longer impossible. The cost is one normalisation in the writer, which must set `until = date` when the user names only one day. Found by the first adversarial review of this milestone, which was asked for the strongest argument against 37 and produced exactly this one; the answer was checked against the database before being acted on. `ExemptionTest::test_a_one_day_exemption_is_a_bounded_range` asserts the payoff in SQL rather than through the model, because the model was never the thing at risk.

Decided 2026-09-11: **the user recorded on a calendar row must belong to that row's agency or be a platform user, and a trigger says so because no foreign key can.** `suspensions`, `exemptions` and `overtimes` each record who declared, entered or approved the row, and each does so with a single-column FK to `users` rather than the paired `(user_id, agency_id)` this schema uses everywhere else — `attestations` does pair, because a signature must come from inside the agency. The single column is deliberate and stays: a platform superuser who has *entered* an agency does the data entry for it, and their own `agency_id` is the platform row, so a paired FK would refuse exactly the person doing the work. But the FK is **wider than that intent** — it also accepts an ordinary user of some unrelated third agency, which nothing should ever record. The application cannot produce such a row (`AgencyScope` and `EnsureAgency` mean the acting user is either this agency's or a platform user who entered it), and that is precisely the argument 07-constraints.md's own first paragraph rejects: the app is one client among several, and the database is the only place a rule cannot be bypassed. `actor_of_agency()` in `0001_01_01_000018_prepare_calendar` is therefore a `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE OF user_id, agency_id` trigger on all three tables, raising P0001 when the user's agency is neither this row's nor the platform one. It is the same division of labour `origin_is_platform()` makes for the other deliberate cross-agency pointer in the schema, and it needs no dynamic SQL because all three tables carry both columns under the same names. It is **silent on two inputs**, each because another constraint owes the refusal and shadowing it would leave that constraint with no reachable violation: a user that does not exist, where the FK owes 23503, and a null `agency_id`, where the NOT NULL owes 23502. The second was not foreseen and was found immediately by the existing `*_needs_an_agency` tests, which began answering P0001 the moment the trigger was attached — a `BEFORE ROW` trigger runs ahead of the column constraints, and `actor.agency_id = NULL` is NULL rather than true, so every one of them fell through to the RAISE. Found by the first adversarial review of this milestone, as the one thing it identified that no question had asked about. A new `prepare_calendar` migration holds the function, which is why the calendar tables renumber from `000018` to `000019`–`000022`; pre-deployment names are a dependency order, not a history.

Decided 2026-09-11: **Milestone 5 ships file import only, and records now what a future pull may not do.** The schema lands whole — `terminals`, `enrollments`, `syncs`, `timelogs`, both resolution triggers, and the immutability privileges — but exactly one ingestion path is built: uploading a device's own attlog export. That path exercises `syncs`, the upsert on the attlog natural key, and database-side resolution end to end with no network, no device, and no new Composer dependency, which is the whole argument for choosing it: push and pull then arrive against a core that is already proven rather than against one whose first proof is a device in a barangay hall. `terminals.protocol` therefore keeps all three values and `host`, `port` and `secret` land unused, because the schema is complete or it is not, and a column added later to a table people have deployed is a migration nobody wants. `secret` carries the `encrypted` cast **from day one**, before any writer exists: the predecessor kept the same value in a plain varchar and then leaked it through four further channels — a `-K <pass>` command-line flag visible in `ps`, a broadcastable event payload, an HTTPS POST sent under `withoutVerifying()`, and the persistent activity log — and a cast added after the first row is written is a data migration rather than a one-line change. Two operational rules follow and are the reason this is a decision and not a comment. **An import never advances `stamp`, `synced_at` or `seen_at`**: a file is not an incremental device read, the export may be any month the operator happened to have, and advancing `stamp` would make the first real pull skip every record after it — the predecessor advanced `synced_at` on import and so could not tell a freshly-read device from a freshly-imported spreadsheet. And **the terminal is never disabled in order to read it**: `downloader.py` called `disable_device()` before reading and re-enabled only in a `finally` block, under a subprocess with no timeout that the job's own 300-second limit could kill, so a killed worker left the device locked out and nobody could punch until someone re-enabled it by hand. When pull lands it must also stream rather than buffer the whole device history, paginate rather than post one monolithic body against a request-size cap, and — if it runs queued — copy its input to durable storage before dispatch rather than holding a temporary upload path that cleanup may remove first. All five failure modes are live in `docs/reference/clockwork-audit.md`'s Ingestion section, which this milestone added.

Decided 2026-09-11: **immutability by privilege has to be restorable by `db:grant`, so the REVOKEs live in `AppRoleGrants::restrict()` and the migrations call it rather than writing their own.** `03-terminals.md` rule 1 — a timelog is immutable, nothing is ever pruned — is enforced by privilege and not by trigger: the app role loses `DELETE` and all of `UPDATE` on `timelogs` except `(voided_at, reason)`, and loses `DELETE` on `syncs`. `.ai/rules/migrations.md` said such tables "REVOKE in their own migration", and that instruction has a hole large enough to void the guarantee silently. `AppRoleGrants::apply()` runs `GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE ON ALL TABLES IN SCHEMA public`, and `php artisan db:grant` re-runs it as a post-deploy step after an owner-role rotation — so a REVOKE written only into the timelogs migration is undone by the next deploy. There would be no error, no failing test, and no immutability; the first anyone would learn of it is a deleted timelog. The statements therefore live in the class both callers share, guarded by `to_regclass` so a fresh install (where `apply()` runs long before `timelogs` exists) is a no-op and the table's own migration applies them at the right point in dependency order. This is the identical reasoning that already kept the `migrations` REVOKE out of `0000_00_00_000001_prepare_application_database`, now generalised: **a statement that belongs to both callers has to live in the shared class.** `TimelogImmutabilityTest` closes the loop by calling `apply()` mid-test and asserting the delete is *still* refused; removing `restrict()` from `apply()` fails exactly that test and nothing else. Two properties make the whole thing work and are worth stating because neither is visible from the code: the app role is `rolsuper = false`, a member of no role, and owns none of the tables, since **a superuser ignores GRANT and REVOKE entirely** — which is why `DB_OWNER_*` must stay unset wherever Octane or Horizon runs; and `timelogs` has **no `updated_at` column**, without which an Eloquent void would touch a column it cannot write and fail 42501. The predecessor is the argument for all of it: it had four independent ways to destroy a raw timelog — a flush verb that hard-deleted, a `Prunable` scheduled every minute, and cascading deletes from both the scanner and its own self-FK — and none of them could have been written against this.

Decided 2026-09-11: **`uid` and `terminals.code` are opaque strings end to end — never cast to an integer, never numerically compared, never trimmed by an accessor, never empty.** `uid` is one column of the attlog natural key `(terminal_id, uid, time, state, mode)` and one column of the enrollment key, so what it is *as a value* decides who a punch belongs to. The predecessor got this wrong in four places at once and each one is a distinct failure: `downloader.py` emitted `int(x.user_id)`, which silently merges device user `007` with device user `7` — two people, one identity — and raises outright on an alphanumeric id such as `A17`, taking the entire read down rather than one row; `Timelog::uid()` cast numerics to int again on the model; `Enrollment::uid` trimmed on **both** the accessor and the mutator while the database join used the raw column, so a legacy row stored with a trailing space never resolved while the interface displayed it as matching; and `scanners.uid` — the device number — was an `unsignedSmallInteger` under a `cascadeOnUpdate` foreign key, so renumbering a device rewrote the natural key of every historical timelog. khronoz stores both as `varchar`, adds no accessor or mutator to either, and rejects an empty `uid` at the parser rather than storing a blank that would collide with every other blank at the same instant. Normalising a device's idea of its own user ids is the enrollment writer's job, done once, visibly, at enrollment time — never silently on every read.

Decided 2026-09-11: **an enrollment's identity must stay correctable, so the timelog→enrollment foreign key defers its update check while keeping its delete check immediate.** Found by a failing test rather than by review, which is the only reason it was found at all: `07-constraints.md` declares `enrollments_reresolve` as `AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE OF starts, ends, employee_id, uid, terminal_id`, and the global FK default of `ON DELETE RESTRICT ON UPDATE RESTRICT` made three of those five events **impossible to reach** — the moment one timelog resolved through an enrollment, RESTRICT refused the parent's own UPDATE before the trigger could repair the children. The design contradicted itself, and `ON UPDATE RESTRICT` lost. The mutation is not hypothetical: a timekeeper enrolling somebody as `1102` when the device reports `01102` is an ordinary data-entry correction, and until it is made every punch that device captured sits unresolved. The fix is `ON DELETE RESTRICT ON UPDATE NO ACTION DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`, which is asymmetric on purpose and works because Postgres keeps a RESTRICT check **immediate even on a deferrable constraint** — verified against the running database, not recalled. So deletion still refuses at the statement with 23001 (an enrollment with punches is ended with `ends`, never removed), while an update defers to COMMIT, giving the AFTER trigger room to re-resolve the affected rows before the constraint re-checks them. The cost is that an insert-side violation now surfaces at COMMIT rather than at the statement, so a test provoking one needs `SET CONSTRAINTS ALL IMMEDIATE`; that is acceptable because this FK is defence in depth against a future bug in the resolver, not a rule the application is expected to hit. A second defect surfaced with it and is fixed in the same breath: `enrollments_reresolve` was scoped to the NEW `(terminal_id, uid)` pair only, and **`timelogs.uid` is never rewritten** — it is what the device reported, and rewriting it is decision 42's whole subject — so correcting an enrollment's uid left every punch the device had reported under the old one still attributed through an enrollment that no longer claimed it, which is both a wrong answer that looks right and a foreign key violation at commit. The function now loops over the NEW pair and, on an update that changed them, the OLD pair as well, running its attach and detach passes for each. `07-constraints.md` said "when `uid` or `terminal_id` themselves change, the function runs once more for the `OLD` pair" and the first implementation did not; the mutation that removes the OLD pair is killed by `test_correcting_a_mistyped_uid_moves_attribution_both_ways`. Left open deliberately: whether re-pointing an enrollment's **`employee_id`** in place is legitimate at all. It works and is tested, but "this uid was mis-enrolled and was always Bob's" and "Alice left and Bob took her uid" are different events, and only the first should rewrite history — the second is two ranges, which is what the range model exists for. The writer will have to choose; the schema permits both.

Decided 2026-09-11: **the attlog column layout is chosen by the operator, never sniffed, and the default is the ZKTeco standard order.** An attlog line is `uid, time, state, mode, …` on most exports, but the predecessor's importer read `uid, time, device, state, mode, …` and hard-required exactly six fields — so the devices this organisation actually runs emit a dialect with the device number in third position. Getting this wrong is not a parse error: every field still parses, `state` reads the device number and `mode` reads the state, and the result is a file that imports cleanly with every punch's in/out flag silently wrong. That is the worst possible failure for this table, because nothing downstream can detect it. So the two layouts are named values — `AttlogParser::LAYOUT_STANDARD` and `LAYOUT_DEVICE` — surfaced as `--layout` on `timelogs:import` and as an optional field on the upload, and **the parser never guesses between them**. Sniffing was considered and rejected: a line like `0001 <time> 0 1 0` is valid under both readings and means different things, so any heuristic is a coin toss on exactly the files where it matters. The default is `standard` rather than the predecessor's dialect because a default should be the format the spec describes, and an operator importing from a device that needs the other one will see the wrong answer on their first file rather than after a payroll period. A future improvement, deliberately not built now: remember the layout per terminal, since a given device always emits the same one — that is a column on `terminals` and a UI decision, and it belongs with the terminals resource rather than with the parser.

**Corrected the same day, before the milestone closed.** The first implementation of `LAYOUT_DEVICE` read *past* the device column and discarded it, on the reasoning that `timelogs.terminal_id` already records the device and the file's copy is redundant. That is wrong, and the mistake is worth keeping on the record because it is easy to make twice: **an attlog carries no ULID.** The device column is the file's one and only statement of which scanner produced its punches — there is no other indicator anywhere in it — so `terminal_id` is not corroborated by the file, it is simply whichever terminal a human picked from a dropdown. Measured before the fix: a file stating `device 7`, imported into a terminal whose code was `3`, accepted every row, rejected none, and attributed every punch to a scanner that never recorded them. Silently. The predecessor **did** make this check (`$this->device !== (int) $entry[2]` → refuse the file), and dropping it was a regression against the one part of its importer that was protecting real data. The parser now returns `device` and `ImportTimelogs` compares it to `terminals.code`, as strings per decision 42.

**Corrected a second time, in the same session, and this correction matters more than the first.** The initial fix rejected mismatched rows *individually*, on the reasoning that a multi-device export should still import the rows belonging to the terminal being loaded. That is strictly worse than doing nothing, and the owner saw it before the measurement did: a scanner exports its own log, so more than one device number in one file means it was merged or altered. Measured — a genuine device-7 export with two forged device-3 rows appended, run once against each terminal, stored **every row including both forgeries**, each run reporting an unremarkable two accepted and two rejected. Per-row rejection does not refuse a mixed file, it *splits* it, and two runs reassemble it. So **a file names exactly one device or it does not import**: the run is refused whole, before a single row is written, with no override and no flag. The scan is a separate pass over the file precisely so "refused" can mean nothing was written, and it holds a handful of strings, so memory stays flat. The refusal is **recorded as a failed sync carrying the reason**, because a tampering attempt is evidence — without a row the second attempt looks exactly like the first and nothing accumulates for anyone to notice. The predecessor refused these files too, with an error saying "likelihood of being tampered with"; overriding that was the mistake. `LAYOUT_STANDARD` carries no device column, so nothing is checked and the operator's choice stands — the limit of the guarantee, with its own test so it reads as a boundary rather than an oversight.

One bug is recorded here rather than quietly fixed, because it is the funniest possible way to get this wrong and someone will do it again: collecting the distinct device codes into an array **keyed** by the device number let PHP coerce the numeric-string key to an integer, so a code came back out as `int 7`, `7 !== '7'`, and the check written to enforce decision 42 refused every correctly matched numeric device. Collect into a list and compare with `in_array(..., strict)`.

45. Decided 2026-09-11: **a `SECURITY DEFINER` function must schema-qualify every table it touches, and pin its `search_path`.** Found by the milestone's adversarial review, confirmed by exploit, and the most serious defect this project has had. `timelogs_resolve` and `enrollments_reresolve` run as the owner precisely so they can write columns the app role cannot (decision 41) — and that privilege is worthless if the *caller* chooses which tables they read. The app role holds `TEMP` on the database, which is Postgres's default, so it can `CREATE TEMP TABLE enrollments`; and the temporary schema is searched **first** for relation names unless `pg_temp` is explicitly listed in the path. Measured on both paths before the fix: a punch dated outside every real enrollment was attributed to a real employee. The forgery copied a genuine enrollment's `(id, employee_id, terminal_id, uid)` exactly and widened only its dates, so `timelogs_enrollment_foreign` — which does not include the date — accepted it. That is an app-role user assigning arbitrary punches to arbitrary people, which in this system is arbitrary pay. Mutation testing then settled which half of the fix carries the weight, and the answer is not the one first written down: **schema qualification is what defeats it** — removing `public.` from any of the three references reinstates the attack, and each of those mutations is killed — while `SET search_path = pg_catalog, pg_temp` survives removal and is therefore depth rather than the guard. It is kept regardless, because it is the documented convention and it is what stops the *next* unqualified reference somebody adds from silently resolving into the caller's temporary schema; being unobservable, it is pinned by a catalog assertion on `pg_proc.proconfig`, the same move Ruling P4 makes for a constraint the primary key masks. Note for anyone reaching for the obvious remedy: `SET search_path = pg_catalog, public` is **not** the fix — omitting `pg_temp` is exactly what leaves it searched first — though with every reference qualified it happens to hold too, which was measured rather than assumed. Revoking `TEMP` from the app role was considered as a third layer and not done: it is a blunt instrument that would break unrelated Postgres features, and it treats the symptom rather than the unqualified name.

46. Decided 2026-09-11: **decision 42 governs reading, not ingest — `uid` is normalised once, visibly, at the boundary, and never again.** The same review noticed that `AttlogParser` trims every field including `uid`, which reads as a contradiction of decision 42's "never trimmed". It is not, and the distinction is worth stating because the two look identical and only one is a defect. What decision 42 forbids is what the predecessor did: trimming inside an **accessor and a mutator** while the database join compared the raw column, so a row stored with a trailing space never resolved while the interface displayed it as matching — a disagreement between two layers about what the value *is*. Trimming at ingest is the opposite: it happens once, in one place, before the value is ever stored, and every subsequent reader and every SQL join sees the identical bytes. It is also not optional — attlog files arrive with CRLF line endings and space-padded columns, and a parser that preserved that would leave every punch in such a file unresolved. So: normalise at the boundary, never on read. `Enrollment` and `Timelog` still carry no accessor or mutator on `uid`, and that part of decision 42 stands unchanged.
